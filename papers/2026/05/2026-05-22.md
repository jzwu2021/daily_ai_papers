# AI Papers Digest — 2026-05-22

Selected recent arXiv papers.

## Overview
Recent arXiv activity is strong on agent evaluation, deployment efficiency, and multimodal robustness rather than pure model-size escalation. Several papers introduce harder, more verifiable benchmarks for long-horizon research and video reasoning, while others target practical bottlenecks such as latency, power efficiency, and retrieval planning. Robotics and spoken-interaction work also stand out, suggesting that the field is pushing toward systems that are more grounded, more continuous, and more operationally realistic.

## Papers
### 1. DeepWeb-Bench: A Deep Research Benchmark Demanding Massive Cross-Source Evidence and Long-Horizon Derivation
Date: 2026-05-20
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21482
Summary: This paper introduces a benchmark for deep-research agents that must search the open web, collect evidence, and derive answers over long reasoning chains. It matters because the benchmark emphasizes cross-source reconciliation, provenance, and multi-step derivation, making it a stronger test of research-agent quality than short static QA tasks.

### 2. Agent JIT Compilation for Latency-Optimizing Web Agent Planning and Scheduling
Date: 2026-05-20
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21470
Summary: The authors propose compiling computer-use tasks into executable code that can include tool calls, LLM calls, and parallel execution instead of relying on a slow sequential screenshot loop. This is significant because it reframes agent latency as a compilation and scheduling problem, which could materially improve real-world web-agent usability.

### 3. PALS: Power-Aware LLM Serving for Mixture-of-Experts Models
Date: 2026-05-20
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21427
Summary: PALS treats GPU power caps as an active serving-time control knob and jointly tunes them with software parameters such as batch size. That is useful because serving efficiency is becoming a first-order systems problem, especially for MoE models whose operational cost can dominate deployment decisions.

### 4. Conditional Equivalence of DPO and RLHF: Implicit Assumption, Failure Modes, and Provable Alignment
Date: 2026-05-20
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20834
Summary: This paper argues that the commonly cited equivalence between DPO and RLHF only holds under an implicit assumption that is often violated in practice. Its contribution matters because alignment methods are widely deployed, and showing where a simpler objective diverges from true preference alignment has direct implications for training choices.

### 5. PointACT: Vision-Language-Action Models with Multi-Scale Point-Action Interaction
Date: 2026-05-20
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21414
Summary: PointACT injects hierarchical 3D point-cloud structure into action decoding for robotic manipulation rather than relying mainly on 2D visual representations. The paper is promising because stronger geometric grounding could make general-purpose robot policies more reliable in cluttered, physically realistic environments.

### 6. Lost in Fog: Sensor Perturbations Expose Reasoning Fragility in Driving VLAs
Date: 2026-05-20
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21446
Summary: This study evaluates a driving vision-language-action model across nearly two thousand perturbed scenarios and finds that explanation consistency closely tracks control reliability. The result is important because it suggests reasoning stability under degraded sensors may be a practical signal for autonomous-driving robustness.

### 7. TempGlitch: Evaluating Vision-Language Models for Temporal Glitch Detection in Gameplay Videos
Date: 2026-05-20
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21443
Summary: TempGlitch argues that many gameplay glitches are fundamentally temporal and therefore poorly captured by single-frame evaluation. That makes the benchmark useful because it stresses video understanding in a setting where temporal coherence, not just static recognition, determines whether a model catches failure cases.

### 8. DuplexSLA: A Full-Duplex Spoken Language Model with Synchronized Speech, Language, and Action
Date: 2026-05-20
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20755
Summary: DuplexSLA jointly decodes user audio, assistant audio, and a structured action stream on a shared timeline, aiming to make listening, speaking, planning, and tool use native to one model. This is notable because full-duplex spoken agents remain a hard systems challenge, and the paper pushes toward more real-time, agentic voice interaction.

### 9. PlanRAG-Audio: Planning and Retrieval Augmented Generation for Long-form Audio Understanding
Date: 2026-05-19
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20414
Summary: PlanRAG-Audio tackles long-form audio understanding by planning which modalities and temporal spans are needed, then retrieving only relevant evidence from a structured store. The approach matters because long audio context is expensive, and selective retrieval offers a more scalable path to cross-domain reasoning over speech, speakers, emotion, and sound events.

## Themes
- Agent evaluation is moving toward longer-horizon, evidence-heavy, provenance-aware tasks.
- Runtime efficiency is becoming central, spanning compilation, scheduling, power control, and retrieval design.
- Multimodal systems are being tested more seriously on temporal reasoning and robustness under perturbation.
- Robotics work is pushing deeper geometric grounding between perception and action.
- Alignment research is continuing to scrutinize whether widely used objectives really optimize the behavior practitioners think they do.
