# AI Papers Digest — 2026-05-24
Selected recent arXiv papers.

## Overview
The latest arXiv batch is heavy on agent systems, embodied reasoning, and video understanding. Several papers try to move beyond generic language modeling by giving systems better memory control, safer multi-agent communication, or stronger grounding in geometry, gesture, and motion. Taken together, the set suggests current research is pushing AI toward richer interaction with persistent environments, real-world spatial structure, and operational reliability.

## Papers
### 1. Which Way Did It Move? Diagnosing and Overcoming Directional Motion Blindness in Video-LLMs
Date: 2026-05-21
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22823
Summary: This paper shows that many video LLMs fail at a basic perceptual task: determining signed motion direction, even when internal representations still contain the relevant signal. Its proposed DeltaDirect objective sharply improves motion-direction accuracy without hurting broader video understanding, making it a useful step toward video models that reason more faithfully about physical change.

### 2. Cambrian-P: Pose-Grounded Video Understanding
Date: 2026-05-21
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22819
Summary: Cambrian-P adds camera-pose supervision and learnable camera tokens to video multimodal models so they can reason in a shared 3D frame instead of treating frames as isolated images. The gains on spatial reasoning benchmarks suggest that explicit geometric grounding may be a core ingredient for more reliable video-world understanding.

### 3. AwareVLN: Reasoning with Self-awareness for Vision-Language Navigation
Date: 2026-05-21
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22816
Summary: AwareVLN introduces a self-aware reasoning mechanism so navigation agents can track their own state and task progress while following language instructions in visual environments. The work matters because better internal awareness can make embodied agents more interpretable and more capable without relying on extra 3D sensors.

### 4. GesVLA: Gesture-Aware Vision-Language-Action Model Embedded Representations
Date: 2026-05-21
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22812
Summary: GesVLA treats gesture as a parallel instruction channel for robot manipulation, helping models resolve ambiguity when multiple similar objects are present. This is important because practical human-robot collaboration often depends on pointing and spatial cues that text alone does not express well.

### 5. MOSS: Self-Evolution through Source-Level Rewriting in Autonomous Agent Systems
Date: 2026-05-21
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22794
Summary: MOSS argues that self-improving agents should be able to rewrite source code, not just prompts, skills, or memories, and presents a pipeline that tests and promotes such changes using production-failure evidence. The reported jump on OpenClaw is notable because it frames agent improvement as a controlled software-evolution problem rather than pure prompt tuning.

### 6. LCGuard: Latent Communication Guard for Safe KV Sharing in Multi-Agent Systems
Date: 2026-05-21
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22786
Summary: LCGuard studies the privacy risks of sharing transformer KV caches between agents and proposes representation-level transformations to reduce sensitive information leakage. As multi-agent systems move toward latent rather than text-only coordination, this work provides a concrete safety mechanism for preserving utility without exposing hidden internal context.

### 7. DeltaBox: Scaling Stateful AI Agents with Millisecond-Level Sandbox Checkpoint/Rollback
Date: 2026-05-21
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22781
Summary: DeltaBox introduces OS-level mechanisms for fast checkpoint and rollback of full agent sandboxes, cutting state exploration latency to millisecond scale. That matters because search-heavy and reinforcement-learning-style agents become far more practical when they can branch and recover state cheaply.

### 8. GS-QA: A Benchmark for Geospatial Question Answering
Date: 2026-05-21
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22811
Summary: GS-QA builds a 2,800-example benchmark for geospatial question answering across OpenStreetMap and Wikipedia, including directional, distance, count, and multi-source reasoning tasks. The benchmark is significant because it exposes how quickly current LLM systems degrade once questions require structured spatial reasoning instead of plain text retrieval.

## Themes
- Video and embodied models are being pushed toward stronger geometric and motion grounding.
- Agent research is increasingly focused on operational primitives such as self-modification, rollback, and persistent-world exploration.
- Multi-agent safety is expanding from output filtering to protection of latent internal communication channels.
- Human-robot interaction research is incorporating richer instruction modalities such as gesture alongside language.
- Benchmarks are getting more domain-specific, revealing gaps that broad general-purpose evaluations can hide.
