# AI Papers Digest — 2026-05-25

Selected recent arXiv papers.

## Overview

This paper set is dominated by a practical turn in AI research: systems are being pushed to reason better about space, motion, navigation, interaction, and stateful execution rather than just produce fluent text. Several papers also focus on agent infrastructure itself, including self-rewriting systems, safe latent communication, and low-latency checkpointing for deeper search. Together, they suggest the field is moving toward more embodied, operational, and reliability-focused AI.

## Papers

### 1. Which Way Did It Move? Diagnosing and Overcoming Directional Motion Blindness in Video-LLMs
Date: 2026-05-21
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22823v1
Summary: This paper shows that many video LLMs fail at a basic visual primitive: identifying signed motion direction, often performing near chance even on simple scenes. Its contribution matters because the proposed diagnosis and DeltaDirect training objective improve motion-direction accuracy sharply without sacrificing broader video understanding, exposing a specific weakness that will matter for robotics, surveillance, and general video reasoning.

### 2. Cambrian-P: Pose-Grounded Video Understanding
Date: 2026-05-21
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22819v1
Summary: Cambrian-P adds camera-pose supervision to video multimodal LLMs through per-frame camera tokens and a pose regression head. The gains on spatial reasoning benchmarks suggest that video models benefit from a persistent physical frame of reference instead of treating frames as disconnected images.

### 3. AwareVLN: Reasoning with Self-awareness for Vision-Language Navigation
Date: 2026-05-21
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22816v1
Summary: AwareVLN introduces a self-aware reasoning mechanism so a navigation model can better track its own state, scene relationships, and task progress without relying on extra 3D sensors. That is important because it pushes vision-language navigation toward more explainable and scalable end-to-end agents.

### 4. GesVLA: Gesture-Aware Vision-Language-Action Model Embedded Representations
Date: 2026-05-21
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22812v1
Summary: GesVLA adds gesture as a parallel instruction channel for robot manipulation, letting the model resolve ambiguity when multiple similar objects are present. The work is notable because gesture grounding is a natural fit for human-robot collaboration, and the paper pairs that idea with a scalable data-generation pipeline and real-world evaluations.

### 5. MOSS: Self-Evolution through Source-Level Rewriting in Autonomous Agent Systems
Date: 2026-05-21
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22794v1
Summary: MOSS argues that agents should evolve by rewriting source code, not only prompts, skills, or memories, because many structural failures live in the harness itself. That is a significant systems claim, and the reported lift on OpenClaw suggests self-improving agents may need deterministic code-level adaptation plus replay-based verification to become practical.

### 6. LCGuard: Latent Communication Guard for Safe KV Sharing in Multi-Agent Systems
Date: 2026-05-21
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22786v1
Summary: LCGuard treats shared KV caches as latent working memory and learns transformations that reduce reconstructable sensitive information before agents exchange them. The paper matters because latent communication could be much more efficient than natural-language coordination, but without protections it creates a hard-to-audit privacy and leakage channel.

### 7. Evaluating Commercial AI Chatbots as News Intermediaries
Date: 2026-05-21
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22785v1
Summary: This study evaluates six commercial chatbots on 2,100 same-day news questions across multiple regions and languages, finding strong multiple-choice performance but materially worse free-response and adversarial robustness. Its importance lies in showing that retrieval quality, regional bias, and false-premise handling can dominate real-world reliability even when headline accuracy looks high.

### 8. DeltaBox: Scaling Stateful AI Agents with Millisecond-Level Sandbox Checkpoint/Rollback
Date: 2026-05-21
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22781v1
Summary: DeltaBox proposes OS-level mechanisms for change-based checkpoint and rollback so agent sandboxes can save and restore state in milliseconds rather than hundreds of milliseconds or seconds. This is important because deeper search, broader fan-out, and reinforcement-style training for agents all depend on making state exploration cheap enough to use at high frequency.

## Themes

- Video and multimodal systems are being tested on more physically grounded capabilities such as motion, pose, and navigation.
- Robotics research is moving toward richer human interaction signals instead of text-only control.
- Agent research is increasingly about infrastructure: evolution, memory exchange, and fast state rollback.
- Reliability work is becoming more concrete, with papers isolating specific failure modes instead of only reporting broad benchmark averages.
- Safety concerns are expanding from model outputs to latent channels, retrieval quality, and operational execution environments.
