# AI Papers Digest — 2026-05-21
Selected recent arXiv papers.

## Overview
Recent arXiv work highlights a shift from isolated model capability gains toward the systems, benchmarks, and runtime patterns needed for dependable AI deployment. Agent papers are paying more attention to evidence gathering, execution structure, and reward design, while multimodal research is stressing perception quality and realistic evaluation. Efficiency work is also advancing at the inference layer, showing that practical deployment constraints remain central to the field.

## Papers
### 1. A Methodology for Selecting and Composing Runtime Architecture Patterns for Production LLM Agents
Date: 2026-05-19
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20173v1
Summary: This paper argues that the real load-bearing abstraction in production agents is the boundary where stochastic model outputs become committed system actions, then organizes runtime design around coordination, state, and control patterns. It matters because many agent failures are architectural rather than purely model-level, so a clearer runtime vocabulary could make long-horizon systems easier to reason about and harden.

### 2. ClinSeekAgent: Automating Multimodal Evidence Seeking for Agentic Clinical Reasoning
Date: 2026-05-19
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20176v1
Summary: ClinSeekAgent treats clinical reasoning as an active evidence-seeking problem, with an agent that queries medical knowledge bases, navigates raw EHRs, and invokes imaging tools instead of relying on prepackaged evidence. The contribution is important because high-stakes agent systems will need to gather and revise evidence under uncertainty rather than simply answer from static context windows.

### 3. From Seeing to Thinking: Decoupling Perception and Reasoning Improves Post-Training of Vision-Language Models
Date: 2026-05-19
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20177v1
Summary: The authors argue that many vision-language failures come from weak visual perception rather than weak reasoning, and show that staged post-training across perception, visual reasoning, and textual reasoning improves results. This is useful because it challenges the current tendency to over-index on longer reasoning traces when the actual bottleneck may be evidence capture and grounding.

### 4. TIDE: Efficient and Lossless MoE Diffusion LLM Inference with I/O-aware Expert Offload
Date: 2026-05-19
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20179v1
Summary: TIDE targets deployment of mixture-of-experts diffusion language models by exploiting temporal stability in expert activations and refreshing expert placement in an I/O-aware way. Its significance is practical: if diffusion LLMs are to compete broadly, they need inference systems that reduce bandwidth and memory overhead without sacrificing output quality.

### 5. MSAVBench: Towards Comprehensive and Reliable Evaluation of Multi-Shot Audio-Video Generation
Date: 2026-05-19
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20183v1
Summary: MSAVBench introduces a benchmark and adaptive evaluation framework for multi-shot audio-video generation across video, audio, shot, and reference dimensions, including longer and less realistic scenarios. That matters because frontier generative models are moving beyond single clips, and evaluation has to keep up with narrative structure, synchronization, and reference fidelity.

### 6. MixRea: Benchmarking Explicit-Implicit Reasoning in Large Language Models
Date: 2026-05-19
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20128v1
Summary: MixRea studies whether LLMs miss subtle but important cues even when instructions are explicit, framing the problem as explicit-implicit reasoning and evaluating it across multiple reasoning types. The benchmark is valuable because it surfaces a failure mode closer to inattentional blindness than to simple factual error, which is highly relevant for decision-support deployments.

### 7. Draft Less, Retrieve More: Hybrid Tree Construction for Speculative Decoding
Date: 2026-05-19
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20104v1
Summary: This work revisits speculative decoding by showing how pruning can free enough budget for retrieval-based compensation, improving the speed-quality tradeoff compared with dense or aggressively pruned draft trees alone. It is significant because inference optimization remains one of the fastest ways to translate model advances into lower serving cost and broader usability.

### 8. Not Every Rubric Teaches Equally: Policy-Aware Rubric Rewards for RLVR
Date: 2026-05-19
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20164v1
Summary: The paper shows that fixed rubric aggregation can be a poor optimization signal in reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards, then proposes policy-aware weighting that adapts criterion-level reward emphasis during training. This is important because many desirable model behaviors are multi-criteria and qualitative, so reward design is becoming a central bottleneck in post-training.

## Themes
- Agent research is becoming more architectural, with growing emphasis on runtimes, evidence gathering, and execution contracts.
- Multimodal progress depends heavily on better perception and better evaluation, not just stronger reasoning narratives.
- Benchmarking is targeting more realistic failure modes such as missed cues, long-form generation quality, and deployment-relevant robustness.
- Inference efficiency remains a first-class research area because serving constraints shape which model designs are actually usable.
- Post-training work is getting more nuanced about how to reward complex behaviors rather than assuming a single scalar objective is enough.
