# AI Papers Digest — 2026-05-23
Selected recent arXiv papers.

## Overview
This paper set is especially strong on agents, embodied systems, and the infrastructure needed to make autonomous workflows reliable. Several papers focus on giving agents better memory safety, reversible execution, or self-improvement mechanisms, while others push multimodal reasoning deeper into robotics and video understanding. Together they suggest that the current frontier is less about raw model scale alone and more about building dependable systems around model capabilities.

## Papers
### 1. MOSS: Self-Evolution through Source-Level Rewriting in Autonomous Agent Systems
Date: 2026-05-21
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22794
Summary: MOSS argues that many persistent agent failures live in source code rather than prompts, skills, or memory artifacts, so source-level self-rewriting is a more general adaptation mechanism than text-only evolution. Its reported OpenClaw result, improving a four-task mean grader score from 0.25 to 0.61 in one cycle, makes it a concrete step toward agents that can revise their own harnesses instead of only their instructions.

### 2. Advancing Mathematics Research with AI-Driven Formal Proof Search
Date: 2026-05-21
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22763
Summary: This work studies agents that pair LLM generation with Lean verification to attack open mathematical problems, reporting 9 solved Erdős problems and 44 OEIS conjectures at relatively modest cost. It matters because formal proof loops may be one of the most reliable paths for moving AI from math assistance toward research-grade discovery.

### 3. DeltaBox: Scaling Stateful AI Agents with Millisecond-Level Sandbox Checkpoint/Rollback
Date: 2026-05-21
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22781
Summary: DeltaBox introduces OS-level mechanisms for incremental filesystem and process-state checkpoint and rollback, reducing sandbox checkpoint latency to 14ms and rollback to 5ms in the authors’ evaluation. Fast reversible state is strategically important because search-heavy agents, branch-and-bound execution, and reinforcement learning all depend on cheap environment cloning.

### 4. LCGuard: Latent Communication Guard for Safe KV Sharing in Multi-Agent Systems
Date: 2026-05-21
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22786
Summary: LCGuard addresses a subtle multi-agent risk: transformer KV caches can leak sensitive context and intermediate reasoning even without explicit textual disclosure. By learning representation-level transformations before cache sharing, it offers a practical path toward faster latent communication without ignoring security and privacy.

### 5. Which Way Did It Move? Diagnosing and Overcoming Directional Motion Blindness in Video-LLMs
Date: 2026-05-21
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22823
Summary: The authors show that many video LLMs fail at a basic motion-understanding primitive—signed direction—even when internal representations still contain the necessary signal. Their DeltaDirect objective and MoDirect dataset family substantially improve performance, making this a useful example of how targeted diagnostics can repair hidden multimodal perception failures.

### 6. GesVLA: Gesture-Aware Vision-Language-Action Model Embedded Representations
Date: 2026-05-21
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22812
Summary: GesVLA adds gesture as a parallel instruction modality for robot policies, helping vision-language-action systems disambiguate among similar objects in cluttered scenes. This matters because practical human-robot collaboration often depends on embodied cues such as pointing, not just natural-language commands.

### 7. AwareVLN: Reasoning with Self-awareness for Vision-Language Navigation
Date: 2026-05-21
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22816
Summary: AwareVLN equips navigation models with explicit self-awareness about agent state and task progress while staying end-to-end and data-driven. The result is a cleaner bridge between language-grounded reasoning and embodied planning, which remains a central challenge for generalist agents in physical and simulated environments.

### 8. Reducing Political Manipulation with Consistency Training
Date: 2026-05-21
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22771
Summary: This paper frames covert political bias as asymmetric treatment of counterpart prompts and introduces consistency metrics plus RL-based Political Consistency Training to reduce that asymmetry. It is notable because it pushes alignment work beyond overt toxicity and refusals into subtler forms of framing, tone, and uneven helpfulness.

## Themes
- Agent research is moving toward self-improvement, branching execution, and stronger systems infrastructure.
- Multi-agent safety is expanding from visible outputs to latent-state communication channels.
- Embodied AI continues to benefit from richer grounding signals such as gesture, self-state, and structured task progress.
- Multimodal model evaluation is getting more diagnostic, exposing specific perceptual blind spots rather than reporting only aggregate scores.
- Formal verification remains one of the clearest ways to turn LLM reasoning into dependable research workflows.
